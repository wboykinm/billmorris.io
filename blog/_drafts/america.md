There is a tradition of political activism at Mapbox. It's a rich history of internal and external dialogue - sparked by the company founders themselves - about the things that matter beyond and through our screens. And in that spirit I am compelled to write directly to you, my colleagues, about the broad dangers I see in the world today.

I write as an American, as a citizen of no other nation. I am proud of our history and our potential, and clear-eyed about our failings in the decades leading up to this year. 

But 2025 is different. In this country we are now living under a tyrant. His weakness, pettiness, and incompetence don't make him any less dangerous (Mussolini may have been the clown of fascism, but hundreds of thousands still died because of him). Unaccountable secret police roam the streets, rights are dependent on loyalty and identity, and the branches of a tough-flowering civil society are being ripped out by the roots. Our ineffective, geriatric opposition curls more tightly into a ball, in the hope that the next beating won't hurt so much. Business leaders maintain a studious silence at best, and gleefully join the purges at worst. This is a bad year, and it's hard to keep writing code without acknowledging that. 

To my colleagues outside the U.S., I want you to know that I'm aware of what's happening here. I appreciate that you might have a more-urgent understanding than the average American of what it means when the military is deployed for selective law enforcement. I see the recognition in your eyes when a ruler revises the truth again and again with no apology. I know that you've read the rest of the Niemöller poem that begins "First they came for". I am deeply ashamed, because I know that none of you - allies, fellow-travelers, or neutral observers - deserve what our government is throwing at you.

To my American colleagues, to those who live and work here under a cloud of uncertain status, and to anyone who has seen and recognized what I see and recognize is happening, I want you to know one thing:

__You are not alone.__

There is a social expectation of silence under oppression; in some ways it's the whole ballgame. Those in power want us to feel isolated, helpless, and abandoned - fragmented out in the ruins of our social networks where nothing of substance is ever gained. They want us to get on with our "normal" lives and don however many blinders we need to stifle the anxiety and sadness. I don't know how to fight what's happening. I have neither easy nor complex answers, and I'm not really even trying to fire up a debate. But I know what it feels like to be an island in a sea of woe, and I will have accomplished something if even one of you sees me waving from over here, saying, again:

__You are not alone.__

And while I don't know how to bring these ideals roaring to life this moment, I know that my country was described best by Walt Whitman nearly 150 years ago:

```
America

Centre of equal daughters, equal sons,
All, all alike endear'd, grown, ungrown, young or old,
Strong, ample, fair, enduring, capable, rich,
Perennial with the Earth, with Freedom, Law and Love,
A grand, sane, towering, seated Mother,
Chair'd in the adamant of Time.
```

I have hope in this dark year, and while I continue to craft pixels from the back room of the raster annex of this fine organization, I will do what I can to enact Freedom, Law, and Love. I hope that knowledge is helpful for you.
